03

Honest backtester

A trading-strategy simulator built to catch its own false positives. It starts from the comfortable version of a strategy, Sharpe 6.94, and removes five flattering assumptions one at a time until the same strategy finishes at −0.19. Letting the model see tomorrow's prices accounted for 94% of the edge by itself.

View the repo →
Open source · 115 tests Python · NumPy · event-driven, point-in-time data
04

Finding planets in starlight

A planet crossing in front of its star dims the light by a fraction of a percent, on a schedule. This pipeline flattens each light curve, searches it for that repeating dip, then scores 23 measurements of every candidate with a gradient-boosted classifier. Planets are 4% of the data, so it is judged on average precision: 0.73, against 0.04 for guessing and 0.18 for the best single measurement.

View the repo →
Open source · 86 tests Python · scikit-learn · NumPy · SciPy

About

I started Numeraldo because the people who struggle most with numbers are rarely the people offered help with them.

That has turned out to be the same instinct behind everything else I work on. In the Strano Lab it meant writing the pipelines that made catalyst data legible rather than waiting for someone else to. At a hedge fund it meant building the back-testing framework from scratch, and learning quickly that the interesting question is not what a backtest claims but why it lies. At Numeraldo it means teaching the session myself every week, not delegating the classroom. I study mathematics and artificial intelligence at MIT starting this August, and I'm looking for work where shipping the thing is part of the job.
